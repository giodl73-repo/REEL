# Reading the Runes - Series Bible

## Working identity

- **Series title:** *Reading the Runes*.
- **Season One:** *All the Lights*.
- **Format:** 12 x 48-58 minute episodes.
- **Genre:** speculative adventure drama, deep-time mystery, institutional
  thriller, and found-family story.
- **Working vessel name:** *Palimpsest*. The name remains a canon decision.
- **Primary alternative:** *Afterimage*.
- **Crew nickname option:** *the Wake*.

## Logline

With eighteen months remaining before an unknown planetary crisis, a temporal
historian and the crew of an experimental vessel enter the final days of
civilizations erased from Earth's deep past. They must determine who correctly
read each world's warning signs while the Cataclysm Council manipulates their
destinations, competes for points, and turns the destruction of whole cultures
into a contest for control of the present.

## The promise

Every mission provides:

- a sophisticated lost culture with an ordinary life worth mourning;
- warning signs that support several serious explanations;
- people who disagree for scientific, political, economic, spiritual, and
  personal reasons;
- a deadline the audience understands before the characters do;
- a choice between saving one witness and recovering one archive;
- a catastrophe that reveals cause without declaring one social faction wholly
  wise or foolish;
- a Council debrief where points are awarded and the next manipulation begins;
- a permanent change aboard the vessel and in the present-day mystery.

The audience returns for the lost worlds. It stays for the people who carry those
worlds forward.

## Central questions

The plot asks: **What is coming for the present Earth?**

The moral question is: **How should a society act before uncertainty is gone?**

The character question is: **Can Jonah witness a doomed world without turning
its people into evidence?**

The institutional question is: **Can experts compete for recognition without
beginning to prefer the disaster that proves them right?**

## Tone

The series moves between epic scale and domestic detail. Continents move, seas
rise, planets approach, and cities fail. The emotional center remains a crowded
galley where people from incompatible eras argue over food, grief, tools, and
whose turn it is to repair the water recycler.

Humor is not a shield against emotion. It is evidence that these people have a
life together. The recurring Council point joke begins lightly, becomes cruel
when Talia hears Atlantis reduced to a score, and ends in silence when every
light activates.

No lost culture is filmed as an exotic ruin awaiting the modern gaze. Viewers
first encounter homes, labor, appetite, status, affection, entertainment, and
politics. Monumental images follow human orientation rather than replace it.

## Format engine

Each episode carries three stories.

### A story: the last days

Jonah and the field team enter a civilization during its terminal window. A
local POV character wants something that would matter even if the catastrophe
never came. Their pursuit makes the warning signs personal.

### B story: life aboard the vessel

The crew absorbs the prior mission's rescue, loss, lie, injury, debt, food,
ritual, or disagreement. This story accumulates rather than resetting.

### C story: the Houses

One or more Council Houses try to select a destination, shape the briefing,
control the retrieval choice, change the scoring rule, or condition Jonah's
interpretation of later evidence.

The debrief joins all three stories. The lost culture becomes a Council result;
the crew contests what that result means; a House converts it into the next move.

## The present clock

At the pilot's opening, the present Earth has approximately eighteen months
before several observed systems cross unknown thresholds. The Council does not
know whether those systems share a cause.

Temporal travel consumes present time irregularly. A seventy-two-hour mission
may cost Earth one hour, three days, or seven weeks. The cost cannot be predicted
until return because the vessel navigates along preserved causal traces rather
than calendar distance alone.

Every episode opens or closes with a revised present clock. The clock is not an
abstract countdown: evacuations, grid hardening, public trust, food movement,
and House authority visibly change as time disappears.

## Time-travel rules

1. The vessel can enter only a **terminal window**: a period strongly preserved
   by geological, architectural, biological, or mythic evidence.
2. A terminal window remains open for no more than seventy-two subjective hours.
3. The window collapses when the catastrophe passes its first irreversible link.
4. The same place and window cannot be entered twice.
5. The vessel can return with its standing crew plus either one additional
   living witness or one bounded physical archive.
6. A rescued witness cannot return to their original window.
7. Instruments and samples lose fidelity in transit. Memories, relationships,
   injuries, and biological consequences remain.
8. The Pattern Engine must select a destination from evidence already present in
   the timeline. It cannot invent a coordinate from theory alone.
9. Interventions can become part of the preserved evidence that enabled the
   visit. This is a closed-loop risk, not proof that history cannot change.
10. Nobody knows whether a changed past creates a branch or was always part of
    the present. The first season answers only the narrower Jonah loop.

These rules force testimony, judgment, and retrieval choices. They prevent the
crew from solving the season by collecting perfect samples or repeatedly
revisiting a failed mission.

## Council scoring rules

- A verified primary mechanism awards one full point.
- A verified contributing mechanism awards a marker. Three markers can be
  converted into one point by Council vote.
- A prediction made before the mission receives more weight than an explanation
  offered afterward.
- A House that supplies the destination coordinate cannot chair the scoring
  hearing for that mission.
- Points determine instrument priority, preparation budgets, and one weighted
  vote in the next destination slate.
- Rescued witnesses may testify but do not vote at the season's start.

The rules appear reasonable. Their incentives are not. Houses select questions
that favor their tools, trade markers, bury negative results in provenance
challenges, and pressure Jonah to produce evidence legible to the scoreboard.

## The vessel

The working production name is *Palimpsest*. It is an experimental temporal
survey vessel assembled around an older, unexplained return chamber. Its outer
systems are modern, repaired, replaceable, and increasingly mismatched. Its
central chamber is made from material none of the Houses can date consistently.

The vessel contains:

- the return chamber;
- a compact field bay;
- Leena's Pattern Engine;
- Mina's machine shop and medical station;
- Kade's sealed Council communications room;
- four original cabins and an expanding improvised witness berth;
- the galley, the only room everyone uses without authorization;
- an archive vault that can hold one retrieved physical archive at a time.

Every rescue creates a spatial problem. The ship was not designed to become a
home for the dead worlds.

## Core ensemble

### Jonah Vale - temporal historian and field commander

**Public history:** Jonah specializes in reconstructing disasters from cultural
records and physical traces. He became publicly notorious after refusing to
endorse a coastal evacuation whose evidence he judged insufficient. The event
arrived earlier than his threshold model predicted.

**Want:** determine the present catastrophe with enough certainty to issue the
warning he failed to issue before.

**Need:** accept that uncertainty does not remove responsibility for action.

**Strength:** he enters another society willing to learn its categories before
translating them into his own.

**Fault:** he can become so careful about representing a culture accurately that
he delays the choice its people actually need from him.

**Secret:** the failed evacuation model used an early version of Leena's Pattern
Engine. He publicly took responsibility and privately blames the system.

**Season movement:** observer -> unauthorized rescuer -> manipulated witness ->
author of the mythic record -> commander who acts without a winning theory.

### Mina Reyes - chief engineer and physician

**Public history:** Mina kept emergency infrastructure operating during Jonah's
failed coastal event. She joined the mission because she considers the Council's
theory contest useless unless it changes preparation.

**Want:** return with something that preserves lives in the present.

**Need:** allow grief and attachment to matter rather than turning every moral
question into triage arithmetic.

**Strength:** she can translate a theory into a shelter, route, repair, or reason
it will fail.

**Fault:** she distrusts explanations so deeply that she sometimes undervalues
the narrow preparation a correct causal model permits.

**Season movement:** keeper of the machine -> keeper of the private People
Actually Saved ledger -> Talia's closest confidante -> author of the response
metric used in the finale.

### Leena Voss - computational archaeologist and Vibe Coder

**Public history:** Leena built the Pattern Engine by connecting datasets that
the Houses kept in incompatible formats. She uses "Vibe Coder" first as an insult
she reclaimed and then as the honest name for her method.

**Want:** prove that cross-domain pattern finding can discover relationships no
institution could see alone.

**Need:** separate the courage to propose a strange link from the vanity of
wanting the strange link to be true.

**Strength:** she asks questions no disciplinary boundary permits.

**Fault:** once a pattern becomes beautiful, disconfirming it feels like an act
of destruction.

**Secret:** House Plasma supplied unlabeled training data to the Pattern Engine.
She suspected an advocacy source and chose not to identify it.

**Season movement:** comic confidence -> first extraordinary success -> public
authority -> false-link collapse -> discovery that the mission contaminated its
own training evidence -> designer of a model that shows its dependencies.

### Kade Or - Council observer and security officer

**Public history:** Kade is assigned to enforce terminal-window limits,
retrieval mass, provenance, and contact rules. He has authority to abort a
mission and relieve Jonah.

**Want:** keep one undisciplined crew from destroying the only instrument that
might save the present.

**Need:** recognize that procedure can preserve a mission while betraying its
purpose.

**Strength:** he sees manipulation, coercion, and institutional leverage before
the others do.

**Fault:** he believes he can remain morally clean by recording other people's
choices accurately.

**Secret:** House Flip supported his appointment because it expected him to
protect a future Namar coordinate. He did not know the scope of its plan.

**Season movement:** minder -> quiet accomplice -> falsifier of mission records
-> investigator of House interference -> officer ordered to seize the ship ->
the man who asks whether the engines are ready.

### Talia of the Athelan League - hydraulic engineer and First Witness

**Life before the series:** Talia maintains pressure gates serving Atlantis's
least prestigious harbor. She is running for a regional engineering seat while
trying to keep her estranged daughter from joining a radical retreat movement.

**Want:** return to her family and finish the evacuation she began.

**Need:** find a form of duty that can survive when return is impossible.

**Strength:** she understands that infrastructure is a social promise before it
is a machine.

**Fault:** she mistakes responsibility for the right to decide which losses
other people must accept.

**Season movement:** local skeptic -> involuntary survivor -> witness enraged by
the Atlantis Point -> advocate for every later culture -> challenger to Jonah's
rescue authority -> first ancient member of the Last Council.

## Ensemble relationships

### Jonah and Mina

They share the old evacuation failure but tell opposite stories about it. Jonah
believes he lacked evidence. Mina believes he had enough evidence to begin
reversible preparations. Their affection survives through argument rather than
despite it.

### Jonah and Leena

He needs her system and distrusts its judgment. She needs his field discipline
and resents that his reputation survived by absorbing blame for their shared
model. The Jonah images make each suspect the other is withholding something.

### Jonah and Kade

Jonah assumes Kade will choose the Council whenever tested. Kade assumes Jonah
will always make rescue personal. They are both correct until the finale.

### Jonah and Talia

He saved her and cannot understand why gratitude does not settle the matter. She
will not let him use one rescue to forgive himself for everyone left behind.

### Mina and Talia

Their friendship grows through maintenance work and disagreement over the People
Actually Saved ledger. Talia teaches Mina that a culture can survive materially
and still be destroyed; Mina teaches Talia that incomplete survival is not an
insult to the dead.

### Leena and Kade

He traces the provenance she prefers to leave open. She notices the House
conditioning he mistakes for neutral procedure. Their mutual suspicion becomes
the ship's most reliable investigative partnership.

## The Cataclysm Council

The Council began as an emergency scientific body. Public fascination with its
scoreboard turned it into a political institution. Each House is simultaneously
a research school, funding coalition, preparation industry, and cultural tribe.

### House Meteor

- **Public case:** external impacts explain abrupt heat, debris, fire, and later
  cooling better than slow terrestrial mechanisms.
- **Assets:** sky surveys, interception systems, hardened shelters, defense
  ministries.
- **Private objective:** establish one legible external enemy around which the
  present can mobilize.
- **Season plot:** secure Hammer Sky, win decisively, and use the victory to push
  a single-cause response plan.
- **Blindness:** an impact can begin a cascade without explaining its later
  glacial, oceanic, and social stages.

### House Sun

- **Public case:** solar variability, radiation, atmospheric chemistry, and grid
  vulnerability connect several ancient warnings.
- **Assets:** observatories, power-grid hardening, satellites, energy ministries.
- **Private objective:** retain exclusive control of the present hardening
  program.
- **Season plot:** claim the Veyr event while suppressing evidence that would
  require sharing credit and infrastructure with Plasma.
- **Blindness:** its grid models treat living communities as load profiles.

### House Glacier

- **Public case:** ice sheets, impounded lakes, rapid melt, and freshwater pulses
  translate distant forcing into regional destruction.
- **Assets:** ice cores, climate archives, upland logistics, freshwater models.
- **Private objective:** turn contributing-cause markers into equal standing with
  the more visually dramatic Houses.
- **Season plot:** form a credit pact with Ocean and trade support for the White
  Sea destination.
- **Blindness:** it can explain the water without explaining what destabilized
  the ice.

### House Ocean

- **Public case:** floods, tsunamis, sea-level changes, and circulation collapse
  are the recurring human-scale catastrophe.
- **Assets:** ports, evacuation fleets, coastal governments, marine archives.
- **Private objective:** direct present resources toward moving people rather
  than proving the initiating cause.
- **Season plot:** win Atlantis, partner with Glacier, and attempt to absorb
  Quake's trigger evidence as an Ocean marker.
- **Blindness:** it sometimes treats the visible final mechanism as the complete
  cause.

### House Quake

- **Public case:** tectonic movement, subsidence, uplift, and dam failure can
  produce abrupt destruction without celestial intervention.
- **Assets:** seismic networks, civil engineering codes, deep mapping.
- **Private objective:** reunify data standards split during its institutional
  feud with Volcano.
- **Season plot:** repeatedly supply trigger evidence and repeatedly lose primary
  credit, pushing it toward an alliance with the crew.
- **Blindness:** local triggers do not always explain globally synchronized signs.

### House Volcano

- **Public case:** sustained eruption can create fire, ash, cooling, harvest
  failure, migration, and political collapse.
- **Assets:** geothermal industry, atmospheric sampling, food-continuity plans.
- **Private objective:** keep present geothermal systems operating while
  admitting that similar systems destroyed Orun.
- **Season plot:** use the Ash Republic mission to protect rather than shut down
  its infrastructure, creating a conflict between institutional survival and
  scientific inference.
- **Blindness:** it has learned to define acceptable risk in terms its industries
  can survive.

### House Flip

- **Public case:** displaced climates, incompatible orientations, and anomalous
  maps may record a change in planetary orientation or crustal registration.
- **Assets:** marginal archives, monument surveys, classified coordinate
  fragments, and Jonah's former mentor network.
- **Private objective:** make Jonah willing to enter Namar before its terminal
  window closes.
- **Season plot:** curate his prior destinations so monuments, sky states,
  migrating climates, failing maps, and the Pattern Engine become credible in
  the precise sequence needed for Episode 9.
- **Blindness:** it knows something happened at Namar but confuses successful
  conditioning with proof of its preferred mechanism.

### House Plasma

- **Public case:** electrical and plasma effects connect solar, atmospheric, and
  close-approach evidence ignored by the established Houses.
- **Assets:** almost no public infrastructure; strong dissident networks,
  unlabeled datasets, and direct access to Leena.
- **Private objective:** force admission as a voting House through a result the
  Council cannot classify without it.
- **Season plot:** leak useful and misleading evidence into the Pattern Engine,
  lose the Veyr credit fight, then become indispensable during Ilyon.
- **Blindness:** exclusion has taught it to treat institutional rejection as
  evidence of correctness.

## House alliances and pressure lines

- Ocean and Glacier share markers until Ocean tries to claim a glacial flood as
  a purely oceanic result.
- Quake and Volcano share instruments and incompatible standards.
- Sun needs Plasma's data and cannot admit the dependency.
- Meteor lends votes to Flip because it expects the Namar mission to discredit
  the fringe seat permanently.
- Flip uses every alliance instrumentally and becomes isolated once its
  conditioning plot is exposed.
- The Chair protects the scoreboard because public certainty keeps the present
  coalition funded, even when the score distorts the science.

## House Flip's conditioning sequence

1. **Atlantis:** establish that monumental systems preserve real observations.
2. **Veyr:** establish that a structure can encode a sky state that no longer
   matches its apparent orientation.
3. **White Sea:** make radical migration of climate and coastline imaginable.
4. **Orun:** place Jonah on two official maps that are both accurate and mutually
   incompatible after rapid deformation.
5. **Ship delay:** isolate the crew while the Namar coordinate enters Leena's
   unsupervised queue.
6. **Hammer Sky:** give the Pattern Engine a decisive success immediately before
   it proposes Namar.
7. **Namar:** make Jonah experience the evidence in the frame Flip prepared.

House Flip does not need to forge a clue. It selects the order in which true
clues become thinkable.

## Lost civilizations

The names below are working translations, not claims about real deep history.

### The Athelan League - Atlantis / Nine Harbors

- **Achievement:** tidal engineering, floating agriculture, elected harbor
  governments, and coordinated maritime trade.
- **Social tension:** mastery of water has become civic identity; retreat is
  treated as surrender to political rivals.
- **Ordinary texture:** fermented harbor soup, competitive canal songs, gate
  maintenance guilds, election graffiti, and teenagers joining unauthorized
  retreat flotillas.
- **Serious theories:** offshore quake and tsunami; gate sabotage; long subsidence;
  an upstream lake pulse; religious judgment.
- **Local POV:** Talia, hydraulic engineer and candidate for public office.
- **End:** quake, structural gate failure, and incoming water combine faster than
  the federation can coordinate retreat.

### The Veyr Commonwealth - the Sky Road

- **Achievement:** continental towers distribute energy, heat, information, and
  public services without household payment.
- **Social tension:** universal provision created dependence on one coupled
  network and a professional class that equates disconnection with barbarism.
- **Ordinary texture:** rooftop markets, public charge festivals, tower choirs,
  and neighborhoods competing over light patterns.
- **Serious theories:** solar instability; sabotage; atmospheric charge drift;
  tower resonance; foreign attack.
- **Local POV:** Senn, a municipal line worker trying to disconnect one district
  without condemning it to winter.
- **End:** a solar-atmospheric event turns the distribution system into the path
  of destruction.

### The Kelda Confederacy - the White Sea

- **Achievement:** migrating cities, centuries-long climate records, and managed
  glacial water systems.
- **Social tension:** clan rights and sacred routes preserve social stability but
  prevent movement outside inherited corridors.
- **Ordinary texture:** movable kitchens, ice-calendar marriages, route races,
  and arguments over whose ancestors named a lake.
- **Serious theories:** gradual melt; impact warming; geothermal fracture; dam
  sabotage; a cyclical sacred flood.
- **Local POV:** Aren, a route judge whose authority disappears if the clans leave
  the map he administers.
- **End:** linked ice-dam failures turn the managed lake system into a flood chain.

### The Orun Republic - Seven Furnaces

- **Achievement:** geothermal energy, public material abundance, worker
  education, and elected industrial councils.
- **Social tension:** shutting the furnaces prevents one risk while causing food,
  heat, and employment systems to fail immediately.
- **Ordinary texture:** shift theaters, communal bath debates, furnace-league
  sports, technical schools, and elaborate retirement tools.
- **Serious theories:** manageable vent cycle; deep chamber migration; labor
  sabotage; atmospheric poisoning; cascading eruption.
- **Local POV:** Daro, a safety delegate whose spouse leads the faction refusing
  a shutdown.
- **End:** an eruption sequence turns a labor dispute into a choice about which
  districts receive the remaining clean air.

### The Nacre Assembly - the Garden Vote

- **Achievement:** grown cities, biological archives, living vessels, and
  ecological governance by broad consent.
- **Social tension:** the consent system produces legitimacy and remarkable
  ecological restraint but cannot accelerate without resembling the coercion it
  was designed to prevent.
- **Ordinary texture:** graft exchanges, scent elections, reef plays, household
  symbionts, and children growing their first rooms.
- **Serious theories:** ocean chemistry; engineered blight; current reversal;
  solar heating; deliberate ecosystem succession.
- **Local POV:** Omi, a vote carrier who can authorize local flight but not a
  civilization-wide retreat.
- **End:** the Assembly cannot preserve the cities, but distributed local action
  saves more people than any prior mission.

### The Karst Compact - the Deep Cities

- **Achievement:** impact monitoring, subterranean agriculture, redundant civic
  shelters, and rapid surface evacuation.
- **Social tension:** centuries of smaller impacts created a permanent emergency
  government that needs the sky to remain dangerous.
- **Ordinary texture:** ceiling-weather forecasts, surface pilgrimages, tunnel
  cuisine, shelter school rivalries, and contraband sunlight.
- **Serious theories:** routine debris season; major impact; military deception;
  sensor manipulation; sacred hammer cycle.
- **Local POV:** Pell, an emergency broadcaster who has spent a career amplifying
  false alerts and recognizes the one real alert nobody believes.
- **End:** a major impact validates the mechanism without validating the regime.

### The Houses of Namar - North Was Here

- **Achievement:** continental cartography, observational astronomy, climate
  adaptation, and geographic civic duties.
- **Social tension:** laws, identities, and obligations assume that north,
  latitude, and climate are stable categories.
- **Ordinary texture:** meridian processions, map inheritance cases, observatory
  romances, equator cuisine, and insults based on inaccurate bearings.
- **Serious theories:** instrument drift; moving sky; migrating crust; rapid
  climate shift; planetary displacement.
- **Local POV:** Varo, a skeptical shelter designer who considers Flip's causal
  theory irresponsible while building latitude-independent refuges.
- **End:** a fictionalized displacement proves something moved; the episode does
  not claim that every House Flip explanation was correct.

### The Ilyon Conjunction - the Near World

- **Achievement:** precision astronomy, inter-city prediction contests, orbital
  engineering, and ritualized publication of uncertainty.
- **Social tension:** prestige comes from prediction accuracy, encouraging every
  school to state confidence more strongly than its evidence permits.
- **Ordinary texture:** public ephemeris wagers, observatory meals, child-built
  model skies, conjunction marriages, and academic feuds performed as comedy.
- **Serious theories:** harmless passage; tidal encounter; debris exchange;
  divine conjunction; plasma discharge.
- **Local POV:** Esa, a junior calculator whose error bars contain the truth her
  director removed from the public forecast.
- **End:** close approach produces tides, debris, tectonic stress, and electrical
  discharge. Several scoreboard lights activate together.

### The Oru Continuance - the First Warning

- **Achievement:** recognizes prior intelligent strata, preserves memory across
  biological and geological timescales, and builds the first terminal archive.
- **Social tension:** knowledge that civilizations recur has produced both deep
  stewardship and fatalism about saving the present one.
- **Ordinary texture:** memory adoption ceremonies, amateur extinction clubs,
  archive gardening, jokes told in deliberately erasable media, and families
  arguing over which memory deserves continuity.
- **Serious theories:** recurrence as law; recurrence as selection bias; a single
  celestial cycle; many unrelated catastrophes; intervention by a future witness.
- **Local POV:** Noma, an archive gardener who wants to preserve one living child
  rather than another perfect account of death.
- **End:** the Oru complete the first warning while Jonah's intervention becomes
  one of its source images.

## The recurring mythology

Every civilization has an image of a stranger arriving before the end. Details
vary: ferryman, thief of names, wrong-handed navigator, physician, star-walker,
demon, or witness. The figures do not initially look identical. Their repeated
behavior does.

Recurring traits:

- asks where the warning records are kept;
- carries a light with no flame;
- offers passage to one person;
- cannot take both a witness and the record;
- points north with the culturally wrong hand;
- leaves before the last sound;
- is blamed in at least one later version for causing the event.

The season reveals that several figures are Jonah. It does not establish that
all are. At least one image predates any mission the crew knows how to make.

## Season One episode board

### Episode 1 - The City That Could Not Drown

**A:** The crew enters Atlantis forty-eight hours before the Nine Harbors begin
to fail. Talia is trying to keep a pressure-gate district operating long enough
for an election she expects to win.

**B:** Jonah and Mina reopen their argument over reversible evacuation. Leena is
desperate for the first mission to validate the Pattern Engine. Kade establishes
the retrieval rule.

**C:** Ocean wins the destination over Quake. Flip quietly votes with Ocean
because it needs monumental records established as credible.

**Turn:** Water rises inside the defenses rather than arriving from the expected
direction. Talia's estranged daughter joins a forbidden retreat flotilla.

**End state:** the crew chooses to remain for the second half of the window. Mina
starts a private tally after saving twelve gate workers.

### Episode 2 - The Last Harbor

**A:** Talia and Jonah trace the failure across harbor gates, offshore movement,
and an inland water pulse while the League's factions use the uncertainty to
block one another's evacuations.

**B:** Talia learns that the vessel can take one witness or the complete gate
archive. She assumes Jonah will choose the archive.

**C:** Ocean and Quake fight over whether the visible flood or initiating rupture
receives primary credit. House Flip acquires a copy of Talia's monument survey.

**Turn:** Talia's daughter refuses the vessel and takes children onto the retreat
flotilla. Jonah retrieves Talia because her knowledge can interpret the damaged
archive already copied aboard.

**Point:** Ocean 1; Quake 1 marker. The Chair calls it the Atlantis Point within
Talia's hearing.

**End state:** Talia becomes the First Witness. Jonah has violated the retrieval
recommendation but not its literal rule.

### Episode 3 - A Current Through Stone

**A:** In Veyr, line worker Senn discovers that isolating a tower will save one
district and freeze another before the predicted solar disturbance arrives.

**B:** Talia refuses the witness berth and sleeps in the machine shop. Mina tries
to reproduce Athelan harbor soup; nobody agrees whether the result is edible.

**C:** Sun expects a clean victory. Plasma sends Leena an unlabeled resonance
dataset. Flip uses the mission to establish obsolete sky orientation.

**Turn:** The towers conduct rather than resist the event. Senn disconnects the
least powerful district because it is the only one whose residents learned to
survive without the network.

**Point:** Sun 1; Plasma denied a marker.

**End state:** an archive image shows a wrong-handed stranger resembling Jonah.
Leena withholds the resemblance from him.

### Episode 4 - The White Sea

**A:** Kelda route judge Aren must declare inherited routes invalid to evacuate
the migrating cities beyond a failing ice-dam system.

**B:** Talia sees her own argument repeated in Aren and becomes the person urging
retreat. Jonah worries she is projecting Atlantis onto a different world.

**C:** Glacier and Ocean activate their marker pact. Flip wants Jonah to
experience climate and coastlines moving while civic identity remains fixed.

**Turn:** Aren saves the city whose route claim he invalidates and loses his own
clan's trust.

**Point:** Glacier 1; Ocean 1 marker.

**Retrieval:** the crew takes the climate archive rather than Aren, at Aren's
request.

**End state:** Talia helps label an archive belonging to a culture she never met.
The ship begins to feel like a shared memorial rather than a survey platform.

### Episode 5 - The Republic Under Ash

**A:** Orun safety delegate Daro must choose between a furnace shutdown that
causes immediate shortages and continued operation through signs of a migrating
magma chamber.

**B:** Mina sides with Daro's spouse against shutdown until she sees the clean-air
allocation model. Jonah waits for a decisive instrument reading that arrives too
late for the outer districts.

**C:** Volcano wants a point without a present-day shutdown mandate. Quake offers
shared data if Volcano will unify its standards. Flip inserts contradictory
official maps into Jonah's field route.

**Turn:** both maps prove accurate at different moments. Daro shuts one furnace
to power air circulation rather than stopping the eruption.

**Point:** Volcano 1; Quake 1 marker.

**Retrieval:** one injured apprentice is returned instead of the industrial
archive, forcing the crew to expand its medical and living space.

**End state:** Kade falsifies the apprentice's mass classification as equipment.
His first deliberate lie enters the mission record.

### Episode 6 - People Actually Saved

**A:** The vessel returns damaged and temporally isolated. No new culture is
visited. A fragment in the Kelda archive implies that the apprentice's illness
may be transmissible across epochs.

**B:** The crew quarantines together. Talia discovers Mina's ledger. Jonah learns
that five shipboard days cost the present seven weeks. Leena's Pattern Engine
produces three incompatible destinations and no confidence ranking.

**C:** The Council threatens to end the program over unauthorized retrievals.
Flip's replacement coupler fails safely, delaying the crew until the Namar
window approaches. Plasma offers Leena a diagnostic patch in exchange for future
access.

**Turn:** the illness is temporal damage rather than infection. Treating the
apprentice requires deleting one retrieved dataset from the chamber buffer.

**Point:** Glacier converts three accumulated markers into 1 point during the
crew's absence, producing the episode's Council groan.

**End state:** the crew holds an Athelan remembrance meal. Kade begins tracing the
failed coupler's procurement chain.

### Episode 7 - Hammer Sky

**A:** Karst broadcaster Pell recognizes that the latest impact warning is real
after a career spent supporting politically useful false alarms.

**B:** Jonah is drawn to Pell's guilt and begins treating him as a version of
himself. Mina argues that identification is corrupting the retrieval choice.

**C:** Meteor controls the destination and expects vindication. Flip supports it
to restore confidence in the Pattern Engine immediately before Namar.

**Turn:** the emergency government attempts to suppress Pell because a real
impact would expose how many shelters exist only on paper.

**Point:** Meteor 1, decisive.

**Retrieval:** Pell refuses rescue and broadcasts the real shelter map. The crew
retrieves the impact archive.

**End state:** the Pattern Engine's successful prediction becomes public. Leena
is celebrated by the same Council that nearly shut her down.

### Episode 8 - The Garden Vote

**A:** Nacre vote carrier Omi can authorize local biological vessels to detach
but cannot order a civilization-wide retreat without the consent cycle.

**B:** The Orun apprentice wants to return to any past rather than remain the
youngest survivor on a ship full of adults. Talia teaches the apprentice an
Athelan children's canal game, then realizes she has begun combining cultures.

**C:** Ocean, Sun, and Plasma each expect the ecological evidence to favor them.
Flip pushes no public theory; it needs Jonah to see that correct action can
precede causal agreement.

**Turn:** Omi refuses emergency dictatorship and releases local authority. The
grown cities die, but more people escape than in any prior mission.

**Point:** no primary point. Ocean and Sun receive markers. The Council treats
the mission as inconclusive.

**Retrieval:** Omi sends a living archive and remains with the last city.

**End state:** Mina's ledger records the season's largest number. The witnesses
begin calling it the real scoreboard.

### Episode 9 - North Was Here

**A:** In Namar, shelter designer Varo rejects House Flip's causal language while
building refuges that do not assume stable latitude, seasons, or coastline.

**B:** Kade proves the Namar coordinate entered the Pattern Engine through a
Flip-funded archive. Jonah insists they proceed because the evidence is still
real. Leena experiences the investigation as an accusation against her mind.

**C:** House Flip's terminal window closes in hours. Meteor lends its destination
vote expecting a public failure that will end Flip's influence.

**Turn:** a fictionalized displacement occurs. Jonah sees the wrong-handed
stranger image being made from his own attempt to direct an evacuation.

**Point:** Flip 1. The House celebrates too quickly and exposes foreknowledge it
claimed not to possess.

**Retrieval:** Varo is rescued. His shelters work although he remains skeptical
of Flip's full theory.

**End state:** Jonah realizes his intellectual journey was curated. Kade finds
the failed coupler in Flip's planning correspondence.

### Episode 10 - The Space Between Worlds

**A:** Ilyon calculator Esa discovers that her director removed uncertainty from
the public close-approach forecast. The average path is safe; the neglected tail
crosses the destructive threshold.

**B:** Varo and Talia argue over whether successful preparation vindicates an
incorrect explanation. Leena follows Plasma's leaked data after promising Kade
she will disclose every dependency.

**C:** Sun attempts to classify electrical effects as solar. Plasma releases its
full archive publicly, risking every source to force recognition.

**Turn:** close approach produces tidal stress, debris, quakes, and plasma
discharge. No single House can describe the sequence alone.

**Point:** Plasma 1; Ocean, Quake, and Meteor receive markers. Several lights
activate together for the first time.

**Retrieval:** Esa is rescued with her uncertainty tables memorized; the archive
is lost.

**End state:** the witnesses demand Council standing. Present-day tides begin
departing from every House model.

### Episode 11 - The First Warning

**A:** The crew reaches the Oru Continuance, which already studies older
intelligent strata. Archive gardener Noma is ordered to preserve the complete
extinction record while a child in her care is denied the sole continuity berth.

**B:** Jonah sees the earliest stranger images and believes the entire mission is
a closed loop. Talia argues that recognizing a loop does not identify who first
entered it. Mina must choose whether the vessel's last safe retrieval margin can
hold another person.

**C:** The Council orders recall. Every House fears an archive that could dissolve
its claim to a unique recurring cause. Flip wants the mission completed but has
lost control of Jonah.

**Turn:** Noma reveals that the Oru found several different catastrophe signatures.
The recurrence is civilization under changing planetary assumptions, not one
universal mechanism. Jonah's light and gesture become part of the archive image.

**Retrieval:** the crew takes the child. Noma completes the warning and remains.

**End state:** the Oru archive was not retrieved, but each witness remembers a
different portion. The Council revokes Jonah's command before the vessel returns.

### Episode 12 - All the Lights

**A:** In the present, the Council tries to force the evidence into one response
plan while solar disturbance, orbital debris, ice failure, tides, seismic
activity, and public panic begin coupling.

**B:** The crew and witnesses reconstruct the Oru warning from conflicting
memories. Jonah must act without the complete archive he spent the season seeking.
Mina turns People Actually Saved into a preparation comparison rather than a
score of moral worth.

**C:** The Chair orders Kade to seize the vessel. Houses trade last-minute votes,
leak accusations, and try to preserve control of their response systems. Quake
breaks first and gives the crew its complete unstandardized data.

**Turn:** the combined model predicts a sequence no single House can survive
politically: several are partly right, and their isolated preparations interfere.
Every scoreboard light activates. Nobody groans.

**Resolution:** the witnesses receive provisional Council votes. Distributed,
reversible preparations begin under Mina's ledger. Jonah refuses surrender.
Kade asks whether the engines are ready.

**Final image:** the vessel enters an unauthorized terminal window belonging to
a civilization whose record does not end in extinction.

## The point gag progression

### Episodes 1-3: institutional comedy

Councilors interrupt one another over primary points and markers. The rules are
arcane but recognizable. Talia's reaction to the phrase Atlantis Point puts a
human cost beneath the joke.

### Episodes 4-6: procedural gaming

Houses trade markers and change definitions. The crew returns to discover that
Glacier converted markers during quarantine, as though history continued as a
league table without them.

### Episodes 7-9: political danger

Meteor converts a decisive result into policy dominance. Flip's excitement
reveals that a House may prefer a mission because it expects the right kind of
death.

### Episodes 10-12: category failure

Several lights make the board hard to read. The final all-lights image ends the
gag without a speech: the system cannot score a cascade.

## Recurring galley line

Each episode contains one meal, repair break, or late conversation in the galley.
The scene must accomplish at least two of these:

- carry a practice from a lost culture into the present;
- change a crew relationship;
- make an institutional decision personal;
- allow humor unrelated to exposition;
- reveal who is absent;
- turn a retrieved object into ordinary use;
- show the growing spatial strain of additional witnesses.

No galley scene exists solely to summarize the A story.

## Atlantis two-part pilot treatment

### Episode 1 treatment

#### Teaser

In the present, eight monitors show anomalies that should not share a cause.
Council Houses argue over which signal is noise. The Pattern Engine finds a
terminal window beneath the familiar Atlantis tradition. The scoreboard is
introduced through an argument about who will receive the first point before a
mission has even launched.

Jonah asks what happens if none of them is right. The Chair answers that the
mission exists to prevent that possibility.

#### Movement 1: arrival

The vessel enters a maintenance chamber beneath a functioning Athelan gate. The
city above is not a shining abstraction. Workers are changing shifts, a campaign
boat blocks a service canal, and Talia is furious that a pressure irregularity
has interrupted her election appearance.

The crew's translation system renders Atlantis as the Athelan League and exposes
the first rule of the series: modern myth provides a destination, not an accurate
description.

#### Movement 2: competing signs

Talia shows Jonah three anomalies: gates drawing water from the wrong side,
subsidence beyond engineering tolerance, and an inland pressure pulse. Her rival
argues sabotage. A temple observer predicts divine correction. Offshore
surveyors insist the rupture will pass harmlessly beneath the sea.

Every theory explains two signs and fails the third.

#### Movement 3: the life at stake

Talia's estranged daughter arrives to steal maintenance keys for an unauthorized
retreat flotilla. Their argument reveals that Talia helped criminalize retreat
because false alarms were bankrupting the outer harbors.

Jonah sees his own history in her and initially sides with caution.

#### Movement 4: first failure

A distant quake arrives. The celebrated outer wall holds. Water begins rising
inside it. The political factions interpret the same result as vindication:
saboteurs breached an inner gate, the temple warning came true, or the offshore
event was weaker than expected.

Mina rescues twelve workers from a gate gallery and writes their names in a
maintenance notebook because the Council report counts only equipment loss.

#### Movement 5: commitment

The vessel can leave safely now or remain through the irreversible threshold.
Kade orders departure. Jonah chooses to stay because the direction of the water
means Atlantis is not merely a flood site; it may reveal a causal chain.

The episode ends as every harbor gate opens at once without receiving a command.

### Episode 2 treatment

#### Movement 1: a city interpreting itself

The League remains functional enough to argue. Talia seeks authority for a
partial evacuation. Each faction will support movement only if the public notice
uses its explanation. The wording of the warning becomes more contested than the
route.

The crew splits: Mina helps the retreat flotilla; Leena and Talia reconstruct
gate signals; Jonah and Kade seek the central archive.

#### Movement 2: evidence and custody

The central archive contains high-resolution records but cannot be copied before
the window closes. Talia's distributed maintenance tablets contain damaged,
independent observations. Jonah must decide whether imperfect independent
testimony can outweigh one complete official source.

House Ocean orders retrieval of the central archive. Flip privately requests the
monument survey instead.

#### Movement 3: the wrong rescue

Talia finds her daughter loading children onto the flotilla. Jonah offers the
retrieval berth. The daughter refuses because departure would remove the only
pilot who knows the outer channels. Talia tries to order her aboard and discovers
that motherhood does not restore her authority.

The daughter asks Jonah to take Talia, whose knowledge may save people she will
never meet.

#### Movement 4: the end of the League

The catastrophe resolves as a chain: rupture, subsidence, gate desynchronization,
inland pulse, and incoming sea. No faction possesses the whole account. The
flotilla escapes the first harbor but its ultimate fate is unknown.

Talia completes a last distributed copy while the archive chamber floods. Jonah
chooses her over the central archive. Kade records the choice as medically
necessary retrieval.

#### Movement 5: debrief

Ocean receives the first point. Quake receives a marker. Councilors argue over
whether the inland pulse belongs to Glacier. The Chair refers to the result as
the Atlantis Point.

Talia, unseen behind the observation glass, asks Mina whether that is what they
call her daughters.

In the galley, Mina tries to make harbor soup from Talia's instructions. It is
awful. Talia eats a second bowl.

The tag reveals House Flip requesting the next destination slate and placing
Talia's monument survey beside a damaged Namar coordinate.

## Season resolution and runway

Season One answers its promised question: the present faces a compound cascade,
not one House's isolated catastrophe. The Council's division of knowledge is
itself a preparation failure.

It also resolves Jonah's first character problem. He does not receive complete
proof. He acts by choosing distributed, reversible preparations whose value is
legible across several plausible chains.

Season Two begins with a different question: if at least one deep-time culture
survived, why did it erase itself from the record, and what did it learn about the
stranger images that predate Jonah?

The crew enters Season Two as fugitives with provisional ancient Council allies,
a damaged vessel, a public response already underway, and a home that can no
longer hold everyone it has promised to carry.

## Continuity safeguards

- Maintain separate ledgers for objective event, House prediction, Jonah's
  belief, local belief, and public Council account.
- A House may be self-interested without being wrong and correct without being
  trustworthy.
- A local skeptic must sometimes save more people than the correct theorist.
- No culture's politics divide neatly into modern ideological parties.
- The Traveler cannot introduce a tool that replaces the culture's own agency.
- Every rescue changes available ship space, food, relationships, and Council
  legitimacy.
- Every archive has provenance and dependency; a repeated source is not
  independent corroboration.
- Catastrophe imagery follows a human decision whenever possible.
- The present clock changes after every return.
- The finale may combine established plants but cannot introduce a new primary
  catastrophe mechanism.
