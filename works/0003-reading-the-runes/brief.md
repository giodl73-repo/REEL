# Brief: Reading the Runes

## Assignment

Develop a series bible for a twelve-episode speculative adventure drama about a
time traveler visiting sophisticated civilizations shortly before their
cataclysms while the modern Cataclysm Council plots over which theory receives
credit, funding, and control of the next mission.

## Format

- **Form:** serialized episodic drama.
- **Season:** 12 episodes.
- **Episode length:** 48-58 minutes.
- **Development style:** storyboard animatic for future pilot proof; no renderer
  or final visual style selected.
- **Working season title:** *All the Lights*.
- **Working vessel name:** *Palimpsest*; unresolved in canon.

## Audience promise

Each episode offers a new lost world, a dispute over warning signs, an approaching
catastrophe, and an impossible rescue choice. Across the season, the ship's crew
becomes a family, the Council Houses manipulate the mission sequence, and the
Traveler discovers that his visits helped create the myths guiding him.

## Tone

Warm, lived-in ensemble adventure under severe pressure. Humor comes from work,
food, institutional rivalry, translation, and affection. The ancient cultures
are peers with distinct forms of technical, ecological, political, and social
sophistication; none exists merely to appear primitive or foolish.

High-level creative touchstones supplied by the creator are time-travel
adventure, identity-changing historical intervention, and found-family space
drama. The work must develop its own characters, rules, worlds, plots, dialogue,
and visual identity.

## Required outputs

1. Series promise, thematic question, and episode engine.
2. Time-travel, evidence, rescue, and Council-point rules.
3. Crew histories, relationships, secrets, and season arcs.
4. Eight Houses with public cases, hidden leverage, alliances, and plots.
5. Named civilizations with internal disputes and local POV characters.
6. Twelve-episode season board with A/B/C stories and permanent changes.
7. Plant/payoff ledger.
8. Atlantis two-part pilot treatment.
9. Season-ending answer and future-season runway.

## Source boundary

BANISH owns the causal-inference game premise, cosmology families, epistemic
labels, and catastrophe-system vocabulary. REEL owns this television adaptation,
its cast, episode architecture, Council drama, and screen-story continuity. Any
change to the underlying scenario truth must be made upstream before REEL treats
it as canon.

## Non-goals

- No screenplay draft in this pulse.
- No final casting, production budget, renderer, provider, or visual-effects
  pipeline decision.
- No claim that the speculative catastrophes are established real history.
- No copied characters, plots, dialogue, iconography, ships, or time-travel rules
  from the stated touchstones.
- No anthology reset in which the crew, Council, and mystery remain unchanged.
