# Comic issue grammar

A `comic-issue` is an ordered visual publication with a complete issue-level
promise, development and landing. Its rhythm comes from drawn moments, spatial
grouping, lettering, page turns and spreads. Panel count is not reading speed;
panels have no mandatory film duration.

Plan the purpose, focal image, drawable moment, reading path and ending beat
before final art. Use grids, asymmetric groups, insets, splashes and spreads
where they serve that purpose. Check real recto/verso placement after book matter
is included so a facing-page reveal does not become visible too early.

Keep clean art, character/prop/effect layers, panel geometry, balloon bodies and
tails, lettering and font references editable and independently pinned. A final
page raster is a derivative, not the only master. Translation requires its own
lettering fit and reading proof while protecting approved shared art.

Review one page at trim, neighboring pages/spreads and the complete ordered issue.
Label proof references by publication/edition/page/panel/utterance/layer IDs;
clean reading editions remain separate from labeled review derivatives. A comic
publication issue is distinct from its defect-tracker tickets.

Reading PDFs/PNGs, accessible text companions, editable sources and printer
deliveries are separate output contracts. Print readiness requires actual printer
specifications, font permissions, preflight and physical proof. A metadata check
or readable digital PDF does not establish it.

See [publication contracts](../docs/publication.md) and the
[workflow plan](../docs/workflow-orchestration.md). This format definition does
not install a compositor, migrate an existing comic studio or adapt panels into
animation. Film adaptations require their own shot, acting, timing and sound work.
