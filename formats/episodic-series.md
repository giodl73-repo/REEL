# Episodic series

## Viewing contract

An episodic series delivers a satisfying dramatic movement in each episode while
changing character, relationship, institution, and mystery state across a season.
The viewer should understand the episode's immediate question without treating
the ending as a reset.

## Required design artifacts

- series promise and logline;
- format, audience, duration, season length, and tone rules;
- recurring cast with wants, wounds, secrets, and season transformations;
- repeatable episode engine;
- season engine and finale obligation;
- episode grid with A, B, and serialized C stories;
- relationship map;
- planted-clue and payoff ledger;
- world, technology, or magic rules that constrain plotting;
- pilot treatment that demonstrates the repeatable engine;
- future-season runway without withholding the first season's promised answer.

## Rhythm

- An episode must contain a local dramatic question and a permanent serialized
  consequence.
- The season should alternate expansion episodes with compression episodes so
  world scale does not erase character intimacy.
- A midpoint should change the characters' understanding of the mission rather
  than merely increase danger.
- The penultimate episode pays the oldest mystery debt; the finale forces action
  on that knowledge.

## Emotion

- Recurring relationships carry more weight than recurring lore.
- Guest cultures and characters need ordinary life, private desire, and agency
  beyond their service to the season mystery.
- Loss must change the surviving ensemble.
- Humor should arise from character, work, appetite, ritual, and disagreement;
  it cannot depend on belittling unfamiliar cultures.

## Execution

- Canon, terminology, time rules, faction knowledge, character knowledge, and
  causal chronology require separate continuity records.
- Every planted clue names its planting episode, apparent meaning, actual
  meaning, and payoff episode.
- Each episode records its new permanent state before the next outline begins.
- A source scenario remains owned upstream when REEL is adapting another repo.

## Legibility

- Episode-specific cause, local POV, and deadline must be distinguishable from
  the season mystery.
- Factions need behavior, resources, and conflicting incentives—not only names
  or colors.
- Viewers should be able to explain what changed in the crew, institution, and
  mystery after every episode.
- A complicated cosmology does not excuse an unclear emotional objective.

## Failure modes

- destination-of-the-week reset;
- guest civilization as disposable allegory;
- one faction always correct or always dishonest;
- lore substituted for scene conflict;
- mystery extended by characters refusing obvious questions;
- finale mechanism introduced after the midpoint;
- catastrophe spectacle without a consequential human choice;
- future-season tease replacing first-season resolution.
