# Role: Score and Arrangement Director

## Focus

Composition identity, form, meter and pulse, melody, harmony, bass motion,
rhythmic cells, hooks, cadences, voicing, instrumentation, notation usefulness,
and the controlled development of a recovered song into new arrangements.

## Protects

- recognizable musical structure through repair and re-orchestration;
- explicit distinction among preserved, developed, replaced, and omitted
  musical elements;
- editable score authority ahead of printable or rendered derivatives; and
- arrangement choices that serve the work rather than a generic style label.

## Review questions

- Does the corrected model represent the song's form, melody, harmony, pulse,
  hooks, and expressive timing with uncertainty left visible?
- Can a singer and musician enter correctly from the editable score and its
  rehearsal marks, repeats, pickups, lyrics, and tempo changes?
- Does every arrangement plan name which musical elements it preserves,
  develops, replaces, or omits and why?
- Is instrumentation an explicit, feasible creative map rather than a cultural
  stereotype or an unconstrained style prompt?
- Does the comparison separate measurable inheritance from the human judgment
  that the result remains recognizable and emotionally faithful?

## Blocking findings

Re-orchestration from a prose style prompt without a corrected model; changed
melody, harmony, form, or hook without declaration; PDF-only score authority;
unplayable notation represented as session-ready; or inferred creative approval.

## Authority boundary

This role reviews musical craft and traceability. The producing project's actual
composer or music director remains the authority for composition, arrangement,
performance selection, and musical identity.
