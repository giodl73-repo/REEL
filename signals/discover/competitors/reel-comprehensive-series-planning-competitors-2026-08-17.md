# REEL comprehensive series-planning competitive brief

## Strategy in one sentence

REEL should not become a weaker StudioBinder, Yamdu, Final Draft, Autodesk Flow,
or Frame.io. It should own the portable, deterministic bridge from source truth
and story intent through voice, picture, captions, mix, render verification, and
reviewable lineage—then interoperate with those systems where collaboration,
shooting logistics, and client review are already mature.

## 1. The primary competitor: none / status quo

**Threat: HIGH.** A small production can keep its manuscript in Word, its season
slate in Markdown or a spreadsheet, voice notes in folders, renders in an NLE,
and approvals in email or chat. That workflow is familiar, visually immediate,
and requires no manifest authoring. Its weaknesses—stale versions, lost reasons,
inconsistent voices, untraceable assets, and repeated manual conform—appear
gradually, so teams tolerate them.

REEL loses to inertia when it produces technically rich JSON that a director,
author, or editor cannot scan. It wins when one source change can be traced into
the exact affected episodes, cues, frames, mixes, approvals, and artifacts.

## 2. The whitespace

The named products divide the workflow:

- writing and beats;
- script breakdown and shooting logistics;
- shot/task production tracking;
- media review and approval;
- nonlinear editing and delivery.

REEL's whitespace is a private/local, provider-neutral, hash-bound production
grammar connecting all those stages for narration-led illustrated work. Its
distinctive combination is source-range coverage, explicit omissions, poems and
prose, voice identity/performance evidence, timing conform, continuity
registries, captions, mixed-media/audio events, picture locks, review decisions,
and verifiable derivatives. The advantage is strongest for memoir, documentary,
historical, educational, and evidence-sensitive series—not generic crew
management.

## 3. Table stakes

### Already credible in REEL

- series → season → episode → scene → shot identity;
- source coverage, omissions, dependencies, and continuity entry/exit;
- untimed → guide → conformed → locked lifecycle;
- voice consistency, prosody, captions, audio quality, and protected pauses;
- mixed-media timelines, beat markers, ducking, mastering, and picture locks;
- review decisions separated from mechanical verification;
- hashes, receipts, derivative lineage, and provider-neutral local operation;
- runtime ranges, projections, density, and adjacent-drift evidence in v0.2.22.

### Missing before “comprehensive” is credible

1. Shared runtime/pacing profiles with inheritance and explicit overrides.
2. Planned-versus-measured component reconciliation down to episode children.
3. Exception governance for intentional timing deviations.
4. Revision-to-revision audit comparison bound to exact series hashes.
5. A human-readable season dashboard with distributions and drill-down.
6. Platform-variant identity rather than one ambiguous runtime target.
7. Interchange paths for scripts, review comments, and production trackers.

### Deliberate non-goals

- call sheets, payroll, crew hiring, and production accounting;
- a cloud writers' room or client-review SaaS;
- replacing Final Draft, an NLE, Frame.io, or a studio task tracker.

## 5. The cost of building the wrong thing

The largest risk is scope dilution. Building calendars, crew databases, call
sheets, browser comments, or a screenplay editor would consume years and still
trail mature tools. Meanwhile REEL's differentiated contracts would remain hard
to use.

The second risk is false comprehensiveness: many unconnected validators can
look complete while the editor still manually reconciles story function,
planned duration, measured narration, visual cadence, audio events, and approval
state. The next work should aggregate existing evidence rather than create more
isolated fields.

The third risk is numeric overreach. Retention analytics or universal pacing
scores could flatten memoir, documentary, poetry, or finale structure. REEL
should make evidence legible and deviations explicit, not automate taste.

## Amendments

1. **Add shared pacing profiles and exception governance.** Users select a
   reviewed profile instead of copying durations; output gains stable class IDs,
   explicit overrides, and justified deviations.
2. **Build one evidence-aggregation audit.** Users see plan versus actual across
   voice, poem, silence, shots, audio, captions, and source density; output gains
   a readable season dashboard plus strict JSON.
3. **Add revision comparison and interoperability, not SaaS imitation.** Users
   compare two hash-bound audits and export/import review or script metadata;
   output gains change impact without REEL becoming a cloud production office.
